A Summer Job

Every summer, Yael, a fifteen-year-old girl from Tel Aviv, works at her aunt’s small hotel in a northern village. It isn’t a big business, but it is very popular, because the food is delicious and the views are amazing.

Yael’s day starts at seven. She gets dressed, has breakfast with the staff, and then she helps in the kitchen. The cook, Sami, is an old man with a lot of experience. He teaches Yael traditional recipes from his grandmother. Yael doesn’t like every dish, and she hates carrots, but she tries everything.

In the afternoon, Yael takes care of the guests. Many of them come from other countries, so she speaks English all day. “It’s the best way to improve my English,” she says. She also writes a blog about the hotel and puts her photos on the hotel’s website.

The work isn’t always easy. On weekends the hotel is very crowded, and Yael doesn’t have time to rest. Sometimes the guests aren’t kind, and she gets upset. Then her aunt tells her, “Take it easy. Tomorrow is a new day.”

Right now, Yael is sitting in the garden with her aunt. They are drinking tea and laughing. Her aunt is giving her a valuable gift: Sami’s old cookbook. Yael knows that she is very lucky.
